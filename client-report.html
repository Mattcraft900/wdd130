Client / Developer Team Project - Report

Client Report:

  • How did you decide what content and images to use for your site plan?
  • Well, since the website I commissioned is an archive, I really already had to content on hand; the point was to store it all. I got the information for the character bios on the "About" page from the D&D group's Discord server.
  • How did you communicate with your developer? Were there any issues with communication?
  • Mostly during in-class time. There was a short time toward the beginning when the lead developer was missing, so I had to work with the junior dev directly for a day or two, but then the lead dev showed up and all was well.
  • Did your developer meet your expectations for your site?
  • Yes! Of course I envisioned something a little more polished, but I knew from the get-go that expectations like that are rarely realistic.
  • What will you do differently next time you hire a team to do a project for you?
  • I would probably spend more itme reviewing the actual delivered product. On this project I didn't spend much time looking it over before the final day and found some bugs, but that's really on me for not checking earlier when they first delivered it.

Link to the website: https://isaacodes7.github.io/wdd130-Hilton/

Lead Developer Report:

  • How did you manage your development team and communicate the tasks to your junior dev?
  • I could've done this a lot better. I basically set up the repository & the file heirarchy within it gave him instructions on how to utilize it, and didn't do much after that. I checked in once or twice during classes but he said he was on-track each time, so I didn't worry too much when I couldn't see the changes he was making.
  • How did you communicate with your client? Were there any issues?
  • No issiues; we had a small group chat set up on Discord where I sent in an update once the home page was finished, and she gave some feedback which I implemented. The Junior Dev didn't post anything there.
  • How did you handle publishing your client's site and sharing it with the client?
  • Through GitHub Pages & giving her the published link. Easy peasy.
  • How was your experience with managing your site with GitHub?
  • It was fine for me. The junior dev had some problems uploading his stuff until the last minute, which caused the child pages to turn out pretty rough (because neither I nor the client were abl to give feedback), but I was able to get it working for him at the very end.

Link to the website: https://mattcraft900.github.io/WDD130-Asvitt/

Junior Developer Report:

  • How did you communicate with your team lead? Were there any issues?
  • So, my Lead Dev Eamon showed up to class exactly once during the entire project, which was the second class after the team assignements were made. I heard nothing from him at any time other than receiving the GitHub link (which was late in coming). There were also hiccups working with the client, Gideon, since he didn't have a lot of his content for the site on his site plan at first, but he put it all in evenetually.
  • Did you know what was expected of you for the site?
  • Kind of? It was hard to tell what Gideon wanted at times, since the content in the site plan and the layout of the wireframe often disagreed with each other, though I was able to clarify these things during class time. And again, I never heard a peep from Eamon, even after asking once or twice, so I just assumed he would take care of the home page & the header/footer while I took care of the child pages' contents.
  • Were you able to contribute to your site?
  • Yep. I wrote everything but the header & footer for both child pages, both the HTML and CSS.
  • Did you run into any issues with GitHub and adding your changes to the site?
  • No, just poor communication, I think.

Link to the website: https://eggallow.github.io/wdd130-murchison/

General Experience and Reflections:

  • What was the most challenging part of this project for you? Were you able to overcome it?
  • The most challenging part was working with team members (the other developer, in each of my dev positions) who didn't care to communicate. (Surprise!) No, I wasn't really able to overcome that, though I could've tried harder in the project I was Lead Dev for.
  • What did you enjoy most about this project?
  • I had a otn of fun being given a wireframe and the content to fill it & being set loose to create it with the tools I've learned. It's such a satisfying/fulfilling process for me.
  • What would you do differently if you could do the project again?
  • Be more... aggressive(?) in my communication, I guess? That's not the right word. Basically, make sure that my team understands I'm taking this seriously and I'm here to help them if they need it.
  • Do you feel more confident in working on real-world projects after this experience? Explain.
  • Definitely. It's not much compared to the confidence I've gained in the WEb Dev Society and some other classes like Technical Teamwork, but yeah, I feel much more confident in my ability to take a web development project from start to finish. I enjoy it quite a lot.
